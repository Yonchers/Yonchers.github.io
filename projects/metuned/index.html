Overview

A race-day information problem became a product idea.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

During endurance racing, the factory instrumentation did not give the driver enough information to diagnose developing problems. Poor visibility contributed to misdiagnosis, downtime, and expensive recovery.

Project goal

What we set out to build

Metuned is an affordable, customizable digital dashboard concept for race and enthusiast cars. The product has to package electronics, remain visible to the driver, handle cooling and wiring, and still be serviceable.

Ownership

My role

As co-founder and Chief Design Officer, I focused on the enclosure, PCB and display fit, physical controls, cooling, mounting, branding, prototype narrative, and connecting the race-team problem to a product that could be explained to sponsors and customers.

Constraint

The design constraint

A good-looking enclosure is not enough. It has to survive vibration and heat, leave room for connectors and airflow, allow assembly, and make the screen and controls usable in a vehicle.

Design decisions

Packaging, cooling, service access, and the driver experience shaped the design.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Start from the driver’s problem

The design is driven by what the driver needs to see and operate, not by what is easiest to place on a PCB.

02

Prototype packaging early

Printed enclosures make it possible to test fit, connector access, fan placement, button spacing, and serviceability before committing to a final process.

03

Use branding to support clarity

The visual identity, panel graphics, and race aesthetic are part of making a technical product understandable and credible.

04

Keep manufacturing in the conversation

Wall thickness, fastening, airflow, tolerances, assembly sequence, and future sheet/plate options influence the CAD from the start.

Results

A stronger path from field problem to manufacturable product.

The result, the main lesson, and the next useful improvement.

01 · Outcome

Problem-to-product thinking

A race-team failure mode became a structured product-development problem.

02 · Outcome

Rapid physical iteration

Printed prototypes made packaging and serviceability decisions visible early.

03 · Outcome

Stronger technical storytelling

The project can be explained to drivers, sponsors, teammates, and potential customers without losing the engineering details.

What comes next

The next useful improvement is already clear.

The next major step is a tighter production-intent enclosure and a clearer validation plan for heat, vibration, visibility, connector retention, and installation.
