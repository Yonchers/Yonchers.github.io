Overview

Our senior design team is building a compact hybrid espresso machine.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

The team set out to build a compact machine that could produce a double shot near industry pressure, volume, and time targets while using a hand-actuated mechanism and an integrated thermal/control system.

System

What the system includes

The design combines the manual mechanism, boiler or heating elements, pump support, sensors, controls, electrical power, frame, fluid path, and an enclosure within an approximately 1 × 2 × 2 ft envelope.

Contribution

My contribution

My work has focused on electrical fundamentals, wattage and efficiency constraints, controls and PID planning, component integration, documentation, and helping the mechanical and electrical decisions remain consistent.

Milestone

The new milestone

The team recently received a SendCutSend sponsorship worth $750 in store credit plus merchandise. That support gives the project more manufacturing capacity and is meaningful outside validation of the team’s work.

Design decisions

Pressure, heat, timing, fabrication, and serviceability affect the same shot.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Treat pressure, heat, and timing as one system

Extraction pressure is not useful without stable temperature, suitable flow, shot volume, and safe control behavior.

02

Keep manual input and powered systems coordinated

The hand mechanism, pump, heater, sensors, and controls must complement each other instead of creating conflicting operating states.

03

Design around manufacturing evidence

The drawing package, BOM, poster, test plan, and sponsored fabrication all support the transition from concept to a buildable machine.

04

Make safety and serviceability visible

Hot surfaces, pressure, water, electricity, access, and maintenance have to be addressed in the layout and operating procedure.
