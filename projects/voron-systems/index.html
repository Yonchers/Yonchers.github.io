Overview

I build and maintain enclosed printers as small production systems.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

Functional parts and engineering materials need more than a hobby printer that works occasionally. I wanted an enclosed, serviceable platform that could hold alignment, manage chamber heat, and recover predictably from faults.

System

What I built and maintain

The Voron work covers frame assembly, gantry and belt setup, independent Z alignment, electronics bays, toolheads, probes, cameras, hotends, enclosure behavior, profiles, and production use.

Troubleshooting

How I approach failures

I separate mechanical drag, belt errors, thermal instability, wiring, firmware, slicing, and material issues instead of changing several variables at once. That keeps recovery evidence useful.

Relevance

Why it belongs in the portfolio

These printers show the full equipment-support loop: assemble, inspect, energize, test, tune, document, run, and maintain.

Design decisions

Reliability came from geometry, thermal control, and repeatable maintenance.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Prioritize geometric accuracy before speed

Frame squareness, gantry movement, belt tension, Z alignment, and probe repeatability set the ceiling for every later tuning step.

02

Treat the enclosure as part of the process

Chamber temperature, electronics cooling, material behavior, and service access have to coexist.

03

Standardize profiles and maintenance

Reliable material profiles and repeatable checks matter more than a single fast showcase print.

04

Document ambiguous failures

When multiple subsystems can produce the same symptom, the notes and evidence from prior failures shorten future diagnosis.
