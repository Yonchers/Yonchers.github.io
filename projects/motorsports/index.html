Overview

We built a race program around a $500 Mini Cooper.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

A student race team has to solve technical problems while also finding people, money, parts, workspace, and time. The car cannot move forward if the organization around it is not working.

Team build

What we built

The club developed the “Notta Miata” Mini Cooper as a race project while creating Cars & Coffee events, sponsorship relationships, recruitment, and a team structure capable of supporting the work.

Ownership

My role

I progressed from treasurer to vice president to president. I helped organize planning, funding, recruitment, events, race preparation, fabrication tasks, repairs, and trackside execution across a mixed-experience student team.

Engineering scope

What made it engineering

The work forced decisions around packaging, service access, fasteners, lifting, vibration, thermal exposure, broken components, deadlines, and safety. A solution only counted if the car could be safely assembled and run.

Design decisions

The car and the organization had to improve at the same time.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Break ambiguous failures into physical tasks

A broken race car is too large a problem to hand to one person. We separated diagnosis, parts, fabrication, access, reassembly, and inspection into work the team could execute.

02

Build the organization with the car

Events, recruiting, sponsors, and budgeting were treated as project infrastructure rather than side activities.

03

Use fast fixes without ignoring safety

Deadline pressure is real, but lifting, fastening, welding, fluid, heat, and rotating systems still need safe inspection before the car returns to service.

04

Create learning opportunities

The club had students with different experience levels, so the work had to be assignable, explainable, and inspectable.
