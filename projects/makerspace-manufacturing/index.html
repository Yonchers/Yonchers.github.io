Overview

I helped people turn designs into parts while keeping equipment available.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

A makerspace only works when equipment is safe, available, understandable, and supported. Good machines still fail users if setup, training, files, materials, or maintenance are unclear.

Hands-on work

What I worked on

I supported 3D printers, CNC routers, lasers, woodworking tools, embroidery and vinyl equipment, CAD/CAM workflows, user training, and shop safety. At ASB Products I also gained experience with inventory, deburr, finishing, and packaging.

Ownership

My role

The work combined technical assistance, machine setup, troubleshooting, training, documentation, and practical support for people with different experience levels.

Impact

Why it matters

Manufacturing engineering includes the handoff between design and production. Fixturing, tool access, material, finishing, inspection, safety, and operator understanding all influence whether a part or process succeeds.

Design decisions

Good manufacturing support is as much about setup and teaching as the machine.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Design the setup, not just the part

A usable file still needs material choice, fixturing, tool access, machine parameters, and a safe operating plan.

02

Teach in a way that can be repeated

The goal of support is not to complete one job for a user; it is to help them understand the machine and succeed safely next time.

03

Protect equipment uptime

Small maintenance and setup issues are handled before they become damaged tools, failed jobs, or unsafe work.

04

Respect the finishing steps

Deburr, inspection, packaging, and inventory are part of production quality even though they happen after cutting or printing.
