Overview

I support appliance and airflow testing in a standards-driven lab.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

Advertised airflow and appliance efficiency have to be checked under controlled conditions. Small leaks, sensor placement, ambient conditions, or inconsistent procedure can change the result.

Hands-on work

What I worked on

I helped construct and maintain an approximately 8-foot-cube room for whole-house fan testing and supported portable-spa and food-heater efficiency testing in controlled thermal environments.

Ownership

My role

My work included the physical test environment, sealing and repairs, tubing, pressure and flow hardware, wet/dry-bulb readings, thermocouples, data logging, test setup, and documentation.

Learning

What the work taught me

Good testing is not only reading an instrument. The fixture, environment, leakage, calibration, procedure, timing, and notes all affect whether the data is useful.

Design decisions

Reliable data starts with a controlled setup and documented repairs.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Control the environment before trusting the number

Room sealing, duct connections, fixture condition, and ambient temperature were treated as part of the measurement system.

02

Repair the setup without hiding the repair

Duct sealant, tape, wood, fasteners, and fixture repairs were documented because they can affect repeatability.

03

Use multiple measurements together

Pressure, flow, temperature, and power information are more useful when the timing and setup are recorded consistently.

04

Write for the next test

Clear procedures and notes reduce variation when another person repeats the setup or reviews the result.
