Overview

ME165 gave me hands-on exposure to industrial robotics hardware.

The problem, my contribution, and the constraint that shaped the work.

Context

The problem I was solving

The purpose of the course work was to connect controls and automation concepts to actual industrial hardware, where faults, interlocks, controller state, cabling, and safety procedures matter.

Equipment

What I worked with

The lab exposed me to ABB robot hardware, Rexroth controller components, emergency-stop systems, I/O, controller interfaces, and supervised setup and operation.

Scope

How I describe the experience

This is academic hands-on exposure, not production ownership. I keep that distinction clear while still showing that I have worked around the physical equipment and operating discipline used in industrial automation.

Impact

Why it matters

The work gives me a more concrete foundation for automation support, commissioning, maintenance, and controls-adjacent engineering than classroom diagrams alone.

Design decisions

Safety state, controller state, I/O, and the physical cell all matter together.

The tradeoffs and safeguards I used to move the project forward.

01

Treat safety state as part of the control system

E-stops, guarded operation, controller state, and supervised procedures are not separate from robot motion; they define when motion is allowed.

02

Observe the whole cell

Robot arm behavior, controller, I/O, cabling, fixtures, and operator actions have to be understood together.

03

Document what was actually done

The portfolio avoids claiming full programming or production responsibility that was not part of the course.

04

Translate coursework into support skills

The useful takeaway is familiarity with equipment states, interfaces, faults, and safety-aware troubleshooting.
